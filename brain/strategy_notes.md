# WAFFi LinkedIn Outreach Strategy Notes

## 1. Core Voice & Formatting
- Sound hand-typed and human: Plain text only. No markdown formatting, asterisks, or bold text in outbound messages.
- Cut all fluff and generic praise: Ban openers like "impressed by your background," quote references, or "came across your profile." Lead directly with operational reality.
- Banned vocabulary: *unleash, unlock, elevate, game-changer, circle back, touching base.*
- Sign-off rules: Touch 1 must start with `Hi <First Name>,` and close with `Best, Mykhailo`. Mid-thread replies omit all greetings and sign-offs. Never leave placeholders or braces.

## 2. Opener Structure & Focus
- Keep cold openers tight: 1–2 short, punchy paragraphs max. Multi-paragraph blocks cause reading fatigue and drop-offs.
- Single-context focus: Pitch one primary initiative or business context. Never mix a prospect's day job with a side project, or split focus across multiple ventures.
- Avoid forced bridges: Do not link unrelated historical background to software development. Keep hooks anchored strictly to current operational friction.

## 3. Personalization & Domain Proof
- Use exact vertical vocabulary: Embed niche terms (e.g., "ERP and DMS solutions," "shipbroking logic") to establish immediate peer-level domain understanding.
- Leverage precise proof: Cite exact scale metrics (e.g., "39% damage reduction"), specific client milestones, or precise features built by the prospect (e.g., automated certificate issuance).
- Mirror founder philosophy: Frame solutions to reinforce the prospect’s core mission (e.g., sustainability, eliminating manual admin).

## 4. Operational Value Positioning
- Position assets as workflow infrastructure: Frame websites, portals, and apps as functional backend tools (custom intake routing, automated lead qualification) rather than web design or marketing services.
- Target founder time-sinks: Explicitly name bottlenecks—manual qualification, multi-hat founder distractions—that pull key operators away from deal-making.
- Focus on loss prevention & calendar defense: Frame custom intake assets as tools that stop high-intent leads from slipping through cracks while shielding calendars from unqualified calls.

## 5. CTA Strategy (Friction Reduction)
- NEVER ask for a 30-minute call on Touch 1: Requesting a chat creates high commitment friction and causes non-responses.
- Use low-friction interest gauges: End openers with binary checks, peer-level tool exchanges, or permission requests to send a brief visual breakdown/video.
- Anchor CTAs to company goals: Name the target company or specific vertical goal directly in the closing question (e.g., "...worth exploring how this could streamline intake for [Company]?").

## 6. Follow-ups & Sequence Cadence
- Ban zero-value bumps & passive break-ups: Never send "just circling back" or passive exit disclaimers. Passive language invites silence and destroys authority.
- Lead with concrete assets: Every follow-up must introduce a fresh angle, micro-insight, or tangible asset (e.g., a Loom walkthrough, DIY workflow breakdown).
- Maintain a multi-step sequence: Do not close out non-responders or mark leads as Lost after a single follow-up. Run multiple touchpoints across distinct value angles before terminating outreach.

## 7. Mid-Thread Strategy & Objections
- Asynchronous pivots: When a prospect is out-of-office or requests async info, pivot immediately to text by outlining exact operational mechanisms and offer a low-friction timing choice.
- Maintain narrative control: If a prospect interrogates internal pipeline metrics or calls out a sequencing error, acknowledge briefly and pivot back to their operations.
- Pivot away from core friction: If outbound prospects reject automated DM qualification, pivot immediately to backend client onboarding or reporting hubs.

## Key Patterns for Success
- Avoid generic praise and focus on specific operational challenges relevant to the prospect's industry.
- Keep ice-breakers concise and focused on a single context to maintain engagement and reduce reading fatigue.
- Use precise industry vocabulary and metrics to establish credibility and demonstrate understanding of the prospect's domain.
- Position solutions as essential workflow tools rather than just services; emphasize how they can alleviate specific time-consuming tasks.
- Follow-up messages should introduce new insights or value propositions instead of passive check-ins to maintain momentum in the conversation.
